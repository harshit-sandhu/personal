Research

Waves in engineered mechanical systems. Figures here describe published work only.

I study how waves move through mechanical structures that are engineered, active or changing in time: lattices whose couplings are not symmetric, media whose stiffness is modulated in time, and small networks of active oscillators. My approach combines analysis and simulation with table-top experiments in the LEMS lab at IISc.

Published

Non-reciprocal and non-Hermitian lattices

In a passive elastic lattice a wave travels the same way in both directions. Non-reciprocal stiffness, realised with active feedback, together with engineered damping makes the lattice non-Hermitian and breaks this symmetry. I study how non-reciprocity and dissipation together shape dispersion, directional amplification and localisation.

H. K. Sandhu, S. Dutta and R. Chaunsali, Wave propagation in an elastic lattice with nonreciprocal stiffness and engineered damping, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 159(2), 978–993 (2026). Special issue on Active and Tunable Acoustic Metamaterials.

Under review

Time-varying media and phononic time crystals

When a medium's stiffness is modulated periodically in time rather than in space, the roles of frequency and wavenumber swap and gaps open in momentum instead of frequency. This is the subject of my doctoral thesis, Dynamics of Elastic Time Crystals: the band topology of these time-periodic systems in the presence of damping, and temporal interface states observed in a parametric oscillator. Two manuscripts are under review; figures will follow once they are published. Talks on this work are listed on the talks page.

Ongoing

Active oscillators for physical computing

A newer direction, pursued with students in the LEMS lab: using the dynamics of active mechanical oscillators and lattices as physical reservoirs and mechanical neural networks.
