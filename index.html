THEME I

Phononic time crystals & temporal topology

When a medium's stiffness is modulated periodically in time rather than in space, the usual roles of frequency and wavenumber swap. The result is a phononic time crystal, which opens gaps in momentum instead of frequency.

I study the topology of these time-periodic bands using single-degree-of-freedom parametric oscillators as a minimal platform, and the temporal characteristic admittance that governs how waves scatter at temporal interfaces. This is the core of my thesis, Dynamics of Elastic Time Crystals, and includes the experimental observation of a temporal topological interface state in a parametric oscillator.

Floquet theoryZak phaseMathieu equationDissipation
THEME II

Non-reciprocal lattices & metamaterials

In passive elastic lattices, a wave travels the same way in both directions. Adding non-reciprocal stiffness (couplings that act differently forwards and backwards, realised with active feedback) together with engineered damping makes the lattice non-Hermitian and breaks this symmetry.

I study how non-reciprocity and dissipation together shape dispersion, directional amplification and localisation. This work appeared in a special issue of JASA on active and tunable acoustic metamaterials.

02 · The Laboratory

From equations to experiments

My theoretical work is tested on table-top hardware in the LEMS lab. These platforms turn Floquet and non-Hermitian models into vibrations that can be seen and measured.

PLATFORM 01

Magneto-mechanical oscillators

Motor-driven rotating magnet arrays modulate the effective stiffness of an oscillator in time. Its response is measured without contact using laser Doppler vibrometry (LDV).

PLATFORM 02

Coupled pendulum lattices

A five-degree-of-freedom chain of coupled pendula for studying discrete modal dynamics and wave transport in a controlled lattice.

PLATFORM 03

Optical tracking & system ID

Camera-based tracking of ArUco markers captures full-field motion. Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) then recovers the governing equations directly from the data.
