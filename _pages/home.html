Research

Phononic time crystals and temporal topology

When the stiffness of a medium is modulated periodically in time rather than in space, the roles of frequency and wavenumber swap, and the medium opens gaps in momentum instead of frequency. This is a phononic time crystal.

I study the topology of these time-periodic bands using single-degree-of-freedom parametric oscillators as a minimal platform, and the temporal characteristic admittance that governs how waves scatter at temporal interfaces. This is the subject of my thesis, Dynamics of Elastic Time Crystals.

Non-reciprocal lattices and metamaterials

In a passive elastic lattice a wave travels the same way in both directions. Non-reciprocal stiffness, realised with active feedback, together with engineered damping makes the lattice non-Hermitian and breaks this symmetry.

I study how non-reciprocity and dissipation shape dispersion, directional amplification and localisation. This work appeared in a special issue of JASA on active and tunable acoustic metamaterials.

A newer direction, pursued through student projects in the LEMS lab: active mechanical oscillators as physical reservoirs and mechanical neural networks. More detail on the research page.

Experimental work

Magneto-mechanical oscillators

Motor-driven rotating magnet arrays modulate the effective stiffness of an oscillator in time. The response is measured without contact using laser Doppler vibrometry (LDV).

Coupled pendulum lattices

A five-degree-of-freedom chain of coupled pendula for studying discrete modal dynamics and wave transport.

Optical tracking and system identification

Camera-based tracking of ArUco markers captures motion; Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) recovers the governing equations from the data.

Tools: laser Doppler vibrometer, data acquisition systems, 3D printing, COMSOL Multiphysics, MATLAB, Python, C++.
